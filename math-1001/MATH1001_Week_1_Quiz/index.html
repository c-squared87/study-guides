    MATH1001 – Week 1 Assessment

    Cumulative Quiz: Algebra Foundations, Number Operations, Equations, and Graphing

    Coverage: Days 1–5
    Suggested time: 45–60 minutes
    Total questions: 25
    Total points: 50
    Calculator: Not recommended; try to work without one.

    1. Purpose of this assessment

    This quiz reviews the material covered during your first five study days. It is designed to identify which skills are becoming comfortable and which topics need another pass before moving deeper into College Algebra.

    The assessment covers:

    • Algebraic expressions, variables, coefficients, constants, like terms, and the distributive property.
    • Positive and negative numbers, fractions, and fractional coefficients.
    • Exponents, radicals, and order of operations.
    • Linear equations, equations with variables on both sides, fractional equations, and inequalities.
    • Translating word problems, evaluating and rearranging formulas, coordinates, slope, and slope-intercept form.

    This is a learning assessment, not a Sophia course grade. The most useful result will be the pattern of skills you demonstrate—not simply the final score.

    2. Instructions

    1. Complete the questions before opening the answer key.
    2. Show your work on paper or in a separate document. Even when you can do a problem mentally, write enough steps that you can identify where an error happened.
    3. Do not look up formulas or examples while taking the quiz. This helps us identify what you can recall independently.
    4. If you are stuck, mark the question and move on. Return to it after completing the others.
    5. Simplify fractions and expressions where possible. Include units in word problems when appropriate.

    Each question is worth 2 points, for a total of 50 points. Partial credit is appropriate when your method is correct but you make a calculation or simplification error.

    Part 1 – Algebraic Expressions and the Distributive Property

    Review: Day 1

    Question 1 – Identify the parts of an expression

    Consider the expression:

    \[ -4x+7y-9 \]

    Identify:

    1. The variables.
    2. The coefficient of each variable.
    3. The constant term.
    4. The total number of terms.

    Question 2 – Combine like terms

    Simplify the expression. Show how you group the like terms.

    \[ 6a-3+2a+8 \]

    Question 3 – Distribute and combine

    Expand the parentheses and simplify completely.

    \[ 3(2x-5)+4x \]

    Question 4 – Evaluate an algebraic expression

    Evaluate the expression when \(x=-2\).

    \[ 2x^2-3x+1 \]

    Be careful with the negative value of \(x\) and the exponent.

    Part 2 – Signed Numbers, Fractions, and Algebraic Operations

    Review: Day 2

    Question 5 – Add and subtract signed numbers

    Evaluate:

    \[ -8+13-6 \]

    Question 6 – Multiply and divide signed numbers

    Evaluate the full expression, following the order of operations.

    \[ 18\div(-3)+(-2)(-4) \]

    Question 7 – Simplify a fraction

    Reduce the fraction to lowest terms. Show the common factor you divide out.

    \[ \frac{18}{24} \]

    Question 8 – Subtract fractions

    Find the difference and simplify your answer.

    \[ \frac{5}{6}-\frac{1}{4} \]

    Question 9 – Fraction operations and fractional coefficients

    Complete both parts.

    A. Divide the fractions and simplify:

    \[ \frac{3}{5}\div\frac{9}{10} \]

    B. Combine the like terms:

    \[ \frac{1}{2}x+\frac{3}{4}x \]

    Part 3 – Exponents, Radicals, and Order of Operations

    Review: Day 3

    Question 10 – Negative numbers and exponents

    Evaluate both expressions. Pay attention to the parentheses.

    A.

    \[ (-3)^2 \]

    B.

    \[ -3^2 \]

    In one sentence, explain why the answers are different.

    Question 11 – Apply exponent rules

    Simplify using the exponent rules. Assume \(x\ne0\).

    \[ \frac{x^4\cdot x^3}{x^2} \]

    Write your answer with a single positive exponent.

    Question 12 – Zero and negative exponents

    Evaluate and simplify. Write your final answer as a fraction.

    \[ 5^0+2^{-2} \]

    Question 13 – Simplify a radical

    Simplify the square root by identifying a perfect-square factor.

    \[ \sqrt{72} \]

    Question 14 – Order of operations

    Evaluate the expression. Show the order in which you perform the operations.

    Part 4 – Linear Equations and Inequalities

    Review: Day 4

    Question 15 – Solve a two-step equation

    Solve for \(x\). Show each operation you perform on both sides.

    \[ 4x-7=17 \]

    Question 16 – Solve an equation with variables on both sides

    Solve for \(x\). Remember to distribute before combining like terms.

    \[ 3(2x-1)=4x+9 \]

    Question 17 – Solve an equation containing a fraction

    Solve for \(x\).

    \[ \frac{x}{3}+2=6 \]

    Question 18 – Solve and represent an inequality

    Solve the inequality:

    \[ -2x+5>13 \]

    Then describe how you would graph the solution on a number line. State whether the endpoint is open or closed and which direction you would shade.

    Question 19 – Identify the number of solutions

    Classify each equation as having one solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions. Briefly explain how you know.

    A.

    \[ 2(x+3)=2x+6 \]

    B.

    \[ 4x+7=4x+1 \]

    Part 5 – Word Problems, Formulas, Coordinate Plane, and Slope

    Review: Day 5

    Question 20 – Translate words into algebra

    A number is multiplied by 2, and then 5 is subtracted. The result is 11.

    1. Let \(n\) represent the unknown number. Write an algebraic expression for “5 less than twice a number.”
    2. Write an equation for the complete statement and solve for \(n\).

    Question 21 – Solve a word problem

    A gym charges a one-time registration fee of $12 plus $8 for each class attended. A customer pays $44 in total.

    1. Let \(c\) represent the number of classes. Write an equation for the total cost.
    2. Solve the equation to determine how many classes the customer attended.
    3. Check that your answer makes sense in the original situation.

    Question 22 – Evaluate and rearrange a formula

    The perimeter of a rectangle is given by:

    \[ P=2l+2w \]

    where \(l\) is the length and \(w\) is the width.

    A. Find the perimeter when the length is 8 centimeters and the width is 3 centimeters.

    B. Rearrange the formula to solve for \(l\). Your final expression should have \(l\) by itself on the left side.

    Question 23 – Identify a quadrant

    The point \((-3,2)\) is located in which quadrant of the coordinate plane?

    A. Quadrant I
    B. Quadrant II
    C. Quadrant III
    D. Quadrant IV

    Also state whether the point is to the left or right of the y-axis and above or below the x-axis.

    Question 24 – Calculate slope from two points

    Find the slope of the line passing through the points:

    \[ (-1,2)\qquad\text{and}\qquad(3,10) \]

    Use the slope formula and show your substitution.

    Question 25 – Interpret slope-intercept form

    Consider the equation:

    \[ y=-3x+4 \]

    Identify:

    1. The slope.
    2. The y-intercept.
    3. The value of \(y\) when \(x=2\).

    Answer Key – Worked Solutions

    Use this section to check both your answers and your reasoning. A correct answer reached through an incorrect or unreliable process is worth revisiting; the goal is to understand the method well enough to use it on a different problem.

    Part 1 – Algebraic Expressions and the Distributive Property

    Question 1 – Identify the parts of an expression

    Given:

    \[ -4x+7y-9 \]
    • Variables: \(x\) and \(y\).
    • Coefficients: The coefficient of \(x\) is \(-4\); the coefficient of \(y\) is \(7\).
    • Constant: \(-9\).
    • Number of terms: 3.

    The three terms are \(-4x\), \(7y\), and \(-9\). Remember that the sign belongs to the term.

    Question 2 – Combine like terms

    Start with:

    \[ 6a-3+2a+8 \]

    Group the variable terms and constants:

    \[ (6a+2a)+(-3+8) \]

    Combine each group:

    \[ 8a+5 \]

    Answer: \(\boxed{8a+5}\)

    The variable terms combine with one another, and the constants combine with one another. A variable term and a constant cannot be combined.

    Question 3 – Distribute and combine

    Start with:

    \[ 3(2x-5)+4x \]

    Distribute 3 to both terms inside the parentheses:

    \[ 6x-15+4x \]

    Combine the like terms:

    \[ 6x+4x-15=10x-15 \]

    Answer: \(\boxed{10x-15}\)

    Question 4 – Evaluate an algebraic expression

    Given:

    \[ 2x^2-3x+1\qquad\text{when }x=-2 \]

    Substitute \(-2\) everywhere there is an \(x\):

    \[ 2(-2)^2-3(-2)+1 \]

    Evaluate the exponent first:

    \[ 2(4)+6+1 \]

    Multiply, then add:

    \[ 8+6+1=15 \]

    Answer: \(\boxed{15}\)

    The parentheses around \(-2\) matter. The squared quantity is the entire negative number, so \((-2)^2=4\).

    Part 2 – Signed Numbers, Fractions, and Algebraic Operations

    Question 5 – Add and subtract signed numbers

    Start with:

    \[ -8+13-6 \]

    First combine \(-8+13\):

    \[ 5-6 \]

    Then subtract:

    \[ \boxed{-1} \]

    Question 6 – Multiply and divide signed numbers

    Start with:

    \[ 18\div(-3)+(-2)(-4) \]

    Perform division and multiplication before addition:

    \[ -6+8 \]

    Then add:

    \[ \boxed{2} \]

    The quotient is negative because the signs are different. The product is positive because both factors are negative.

    Question 7 – Simplify a fraction

    Start with:

    \[ \frac{18}{24} \]

    The greatest common factor of 18 and 24 is 6. Divide the numerator and denominator by 6:

    \[ \frac{18\div6}{24\div6}=\frac{3}{4} \]

    Answer: \(\boxed{\frac{3}{4}}\)

    Question 8 – Subtract fractions

    Start with:

    \[ \frac{5}{6}-\frac{1}{4} \]

    The least common denominator of 6 and 4 is 12. Rewrite both fractions:

    \[ \frac{5}{6}=\frac{10}{12} \qquad\text{and}\qquad \frac{1}{4}=\frac{3}{12} \]

    Subtract the numerators and keep the denominator:

    \[ \frac{10}{12}-\frac{3}{12}=\frac{7}{12} \]

    Answer: \(\boxed{\frac{7}{12}}\)

    Question 9 – Fraction operations and fractional coefficients

    A. Divide the fractions.

    Start with:

    \[ \frac{3}{5}\div\frac{9}{10} \]

    To divide by a fraction, multiply by its reciprocal:

    \[ \frac{3}{5}\times\frac{10}{9} \]

    Multiply across:

    \[ \frac{30}{45} \]

    Simplify by dividing the numerator and denominator by 15:

    \[ \boxed{\frac{2}{3}} \]

    B. Combine the like terms.

    Start with:

    \[ \frac{1}{2}x+\frac{3}{4}x \]

    Use a common denominator of 4 for the coefficients:

    \[ \frac{2}{4}x+\frac{3}{4}x \]

    Add the coefficients:

    \[ \boxed{\frac{5}{4}x} \]

    The variable portion stays the same because both terms contain \(x\).

    Part 3 – Exponents, Radicals, and Order of Operations

    Question 10 – Negative numbers and exponents

    A.

    \[ (-3)^2=(-3)(-3)=\boxed{9} \]

    The parentheses make the entire negative number the base.

    B.

    \[ -3^2=-(3^2)=-(9)=\boxed{-9} \]

    Without parentheses, the exponent applies to 3 first. The negative sign is applied afterward. That is why the two expressions have different answers.

    Question 11 – Apply exponent rules

    Start with:

    \[ \frac{x^4\cdot x^3}{x^2} \]

    When multiplying powers with the same base, add exponents. When dividing powers with the same base, subtract exponents:

    \[ x^{4+3-2} \]

    Simplify the exponent:

    \[ \boxed{x^5} \]

    This assumes \(x\ne0\), because the original expression has \(x^2\) in the denominator.

    Question 12 – Zero and negative exponents

    Start with:

    \[ 5^0+2^{-2} \]

    A nonzero base raised to the zero power equals 1:

    \[ 5^0=1 \]

    A negative exponent means take the reciprocal:

    \[ 2^{-2}=\frac{1}{2^2}=\frac{1}{4} \]

    Add:

    \[ 1+\frac{1}{4}=\boxed{\frac{5}{4}} \]

    Question 13 – Simplify a radical

    Start with:

    \[ \sqrt{72} \]

    Find a perfect-square factor of 72. Since \(72=36\times2\), rewrite the radical:

    \[ \sqrt{36\times2} \]

    Separate the square roots:

    \[ \sqrt{36}\times\sqrt{2} \]

    Evaluate the perfect square:

    \[ \boxed{6\sqrt{2}} \]

    The factor 2 remains inside the radical because it has no perfect-square factor greater than 1.

    Question 14 – Order of operations

    Start with:

    \[ 18\div3+2^2\times(5-3) \]

    First evaluate the parentheses:

    \[ 18\div3+2^2\times2 \]

    Evaluate the exponent:

    \[ 18\div3+4\times2 \]

    Perform division and multiplication from left to right:

    \[ 6+8 \]

    Add:

    \[ \boxed{14} \]

    Part 4 – Linear Equations and Inequalities

    Question 15 – Solve a two-step equation

    Start with:

    \[ 4x-7=17 \]

    Add 7 to both sides:

    \[ 4x=24 \]

    Divide both sides by 4:

    \[ \boxed{x=6} \]

    Check by substituting 6 into the original equation:

    \[ 4(6)-7=24-7=17 \]

    The left side equals the right side, so the solution is correct.

    Question 16 – Solve an equation with variables on both sides

    Start with:

    \[ 3(2x-1)=4x+9 \]

    Distribute 3:

    \[ 6x-3=4x+9 \]

    Subtract \(4x\) from both sides:

    \[ 2x-3=9 \]

    Add 3 to both sides:

    \[ 2x=12 \]

    Divide by 2:

    \[ \boxed{x=6} \]

    Question 17 – Solve an equation containing a fraction

    Start with:

    \[ \frac{x}{3}+2=6 \]

    Subtract 2 from both sides:

    \[ \frac{x}{3}=4 \]

    Multiply both sides by 3:

    \[ \boxed{x=12} \]

    Check: \(12\div3+2=4+2=6\), so the solution works.

    Question 18 – Solve and represent an inequality

    Start with:

    \[ -2x+5>13 \]

    Subtract 5 from both sides:

    \[ -2x>8 \]

    Divide both sides by \(-2\). When you divide an inequality by a negative number, reverse the inequality symbol.

    \[ \boxed{x<-4} \]

    On a number line, use an open circle at \(-4\), because \(-4\) is not included. Shade to the left, because the solution includes numbers smaller than \(-4\).

    Question 19 – Identify the number of solutions

    A.

    \[ 2(x+3)=2x+6 \]

    Distribute the 2 on the left:

    \[ 2x+6=2x+6 \]

    Both sides are identical. The equation is true for every value of \(x\).

    Answer: Infinitely many solutions. This type of equation is called an identity.

    B.

    \[ 4x+7=4x+1 \]

    Subtract \(4x\) from both sides:

    \[ 7=1 \]

    This statement is never true, regardless of the value of \(x\).

    Answer: No solution.

    Part 5 – Word Problems, Formulas, Coordinate Plane, and Slope

    Question 20 – Translate words into algebra

    Let \(n\) represent the unknown number.

    “Twice a number” is \(2n\). “Five less than” means subtract 5:

    \[ \boxed{2n-5} \]

    The complete statement says the result is 11, so write:

    \[ 2n-5=11 \]

    Add 5 to both sides:

    \[ 2n=16 \]

    Divide by 2:

    \[ \boxed{n=8} \]

    Check: Twice 8 is 16, and 5 less than 16 is 11.

    Question 21 – Solve a word problem

    Let \(c\) represent the number of classes.

    The gym charges $12 once, plus $8 for each class. The total is $44:

    \[ 12+8c=44 \]

    Subtract 12 from both sides:

    \[ 8c=32 \]

    Divide by 8:

    \[ \boxed{c=4} \]

    Check the total:

    \[ 12+8(4)=12+32=44 \]

    Answer: The customer attended 4 classes.

    Question 22 – Evaluate and rearrange a formula

    The formula is:

    \[ P=2l+2w \]

    A. Find the perimeter. Substitute \(l=8\) and \(w=3\):

    \[ P=2(8)+2(3) \]

    Multiply:

    \[ P=16+6=\boxed{22\text{ cm}} \]

    B. Solve the formula for \(l\). Start with:

    \[ P=2l+2w \]

    Subtract \(2w\) from both sides:

    \[ P-2w=2l \]

    Divide both sides by 2:

    \[ \boxed{l=\frac{P-2w}{2}} \]

    An equivalent answer is \(l=\frac{P}{2}-w\).

    Question 23 – Identify a quadrant

    The point is:

    \[ (-3,2) \]

    The x-coordinate is negative and the y-coordinate is positive. This places the point in Quadrant II.

    Answer: B. Quadrant II.

    The point is 3 units to the left of the y-axis and 2 units above the x-axis.

    Question 24 – Calculate slope from two points

    Use the slope formula:

    \[ m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1} \]

    The points are \((-1,2)\) and \((3,10)\). Substitute:

    \[ m=\frac{10-2}{3-(-1)} \]

    Simplify the numerator and denominator:

    \[ m=\frac{8}{4} \]
    \[ \boxed{m=2} \]

    The line rises 2 units for every 1 unit it moves to the right.

    Question 25 – Interpret slope-intercept form

    The equation is:

    \[ y=-3x+4 \]

    Slope-intercept form is:

    \[ y=mx+b \]

    Here, \(m\) is the slope and \(b\) is the y-intercept.

    1. Slope: \(\boxed{-3}\).
    2. Y-intercept: \(\boxed{4}\), or the point \((0,4)\).
    3. When \(x=2\): Substitute 2 for \(x\):
    \[ y=-3(2)+4=-6+4=\boxed{-2} \]

    Score Guide and Review Plan

    1. Calculate your score

    There are 25 questions worth 2 points each, for a maximum score of 50 points. Award yourself partial credit when your setup and method are correct but you make a small arithmetic or simplification error.

    Score Percentage Suggested interpretation
    45–50 90–100% Strong foundation. Review any missed questions, then continue.
    40–44 80–88% Good progress. Review the specific concepts behind missed questions.
    35–39 70–78% Developing foundation. Revisit the relevant lessons and practice before advancing.
    30–34 60–68% Several concepts need reinforcement. Spend additional time on the weaker sections.
    0–29 Below 60% Use this as a map for review. Work through the relevant Day 1–5 material again before moving on.

    These ranges are study recommendations, not official Sophia grading thresholds.

    2. Review by section

    Use your section results to decide what to revisit:

    Section Questions Main topics to review
    Part 1 1–4 Variables, terms, combining like terms, distribution, substitution
    Part 2 5–9 Signed-number rules, fraction simplification, fraction operations, fractional coefficients
    Part 3 10–14 Exponent rules, negative bases, zero and negative exponents, radicals, order of operations
    Part 4 15–19 Inverse operations, multi-step equations, fractions in equations, inequalities, solution types
    Part 5 20–25 Translating words, word problems, formulas, quadrants, slope, slope-intercept form

    A section score can reveal more than the overall score. For example, a strong result in Parts 1–3 with difficulty in Parts 4–5 suggests that calculation skills are developing well but applying them to equations and real-world situations needs more practice.

    3. Record your mistakes

    For each missed or uncertain question, record:

    Question Concept What caused the difficulty? What I will practice
    Example: 18 Inequalities Forgot to reverse the sign when dividing by a negative Solve five more inequalities

    Try to identify the type of mistake rather than simply writing “I got it wrong.” Common categories include:

    • Concept gap: I did not understand which rule to use.
    • Process gap: I knew the rule but did not know how to apply it.
    • Sign error: I lost or changed a positive or negative sign.
    • Arithmetic error: My setup was correct, but I calculated incorrectly.
    • Notation error: I understood the idea but wrote the expression incorrectly.
    • Careless error: I skipped a step or misread the question.

    4. Weekly reflection

    After grading the quiz, answer these questions in your study notes:

    1. Which section felt easiest, and why?
    2. Which section took the most time?
    3. Which mistakes were caused by forgetting a rule, and which were caused by calculation errors?
    4. What are the three concepts you most want to review?
    5. Do you feel ready to begin the next stage of College Algebra? What would make you more confident?

    Most important: Do not treat the score as a judgment of your mathematical ability. Use it to decide what to practice next. The purpose of this first week was to rebuild foundational skills, and this assessment gives us a concrete picture of what is sticking and what needs more work.
