## 2. Working with positive and negative numbers

Positive and negative numbers are often called **signed numbers**. A positive number is greater than zero; a negative number is less than zero.

A number line can help you visualize them:

```text
<--- negative numbers ---  -3  -2  -1  0  1  2  3  --- positive numbers --->
```

Moving to the right means the value increases. Moving to the left means the value decreases.

### Adding signed numbers

There are two main cases.

**Case 1: The signs are the same.** Add the absolute values and keep the common sign.

\[
(-4)+(-3)=-(4+3)=-7
\]

\[
5+8=13
\]

**Case 2: The signs are different.** Subtract the smaller absolute value from the larger absolute value. Keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value.

\[
-9+4=-(9-4)=-5
\]

\[
-3+10=+(10-3)=7
\]

The absolute value of a number is its distance from zero. For example, the absolute value of \(-9\) is 9, written \(|-9|=9\).

### Worked example 1: Adding signed numbers

Calculate:

\[
-7+12
\]

Step 1: The signs are different.

Step 2: Subtract the absolute values: \(12-7=5\).

Step 3: Keep the sign of the number with the larger absolute value. Since 12 is positive, the answer is positive.

\[
\boxed{5}
\]

### Subtracting signed numbers

A reliable way to subtract signed numbers is to **add the opposite**. Change subtraction into addition, then change the sign of the number being subtracted.

\[
a-b=a+(-b)
\]

For example:

\[
8-(-3)=8+3=11
\]

Subtracting a negative is the same as adding a positive.

Another example:

\[
-5-7=-5+(-7)=-12
\]

### Worked example 2: Subtracting signed numbers

Calculate:

\[
6-(-9)
\]

Step 1: Change subtraction into addition.

\[
6+9
\]

Step 2: Add.

\[
\boxed{15}
\]

### Multiplying and dividing signed numbers

For multiplication and division, use the same sign pattern:

| Signs | Result |
|---|---|
| Positive with positive | Positive |
| Negative with negative | Positive |
| Positive with negative | Negative |
| Negative with positive | Negative |

In other words:

- **Same signs produce a positive result.**
- **Different signs produce a negative result.**

Examples:

\[
(-4)(-3)=12
\]

\[
(-4)(3)=-12
\]

\[
18\div(-3)=-6
\]

\[
(-20)\div(-5)=4
\]

The sign rule applies whether you are multiplying or dividing. It does not tell you the size of the answer; you still calculate using the absolute values.

### Worked example 3: Multiplying and dividing signed numbers

Calculate both:

\[
(-6)(4) \qquad 30\div(-5)
\]

For the multiplication, the signs are different, so the result is negative. Since \(6\times4=24\):

\[
(-6)(4)=\boxed{-24}
\]

For the division, the signs are also different, so the result is negative. Since \(30\div5=6\):

\[
30\div(-5)=\boxed{-6}
\]

**Pause and remember:** A negative divided by a positive is negative, just like a positive divided by a negative. Either way, different signs produce a negative result.

## 3. Fraction foundations

A fraction represents part of a whole or a division. In the fraction

\[
\frac{3}{4}
\]

the top number, 3, is the **numerator**. The bottom number, 4, is the **denominator**. The denominator cannot be zero because division by zero is undefined.

### Equivalent fractions

Fractions can look different while representing the same value. Multiply or divide the numerator and denominator by the same nonzero number to make an equivalent fraction.

\[
\frac{1}{2}=\frac{2}{4}=\frac{3}{6}=\frac{4}{8}
\]

### Simplifying fractions

To simplify a fraction, divide the numerator and denominator by a common factor. A fraction is in **lowest terms** when the numerator and denominator have no common factor greater than 1.

### Worked example 4: Simplifying a fraction

Simplify:

\[
\frac{6}{8}
\]

Step 1: Find a number that divides evenly into both 6 and 8. Both are divisible by 2.

Step 2: Divide the numerator and denominator by 2.

\[
\frac{6\div2}{8\div2}=\frac{3}{4}
\]

Final answer:

\[
\boxed{\frac{3}{4}}
\]

For the fraction from your diagnostic:

\[
\frac{3}{6}=\frac{3\div3}{6\div3}=\boxed{\frac{1}{2}}
\]

You had correctly calculated the numerator and denominator in Question 6; the final step was simplifying the fraction.

## 4. Adding and subtracting fractions

### Fractions with the same denominator

When denominators are the same, add or subtract the numerators and keep the denominator.

\[
\frac{2}{7}+\frac{3}{7}=\frac{2+3}{7}=\frac{5}{7}
\]

Do **not** add the denominators. The denominator describes the size of the pieces, and that size does not change when you combine the pieces.

### Fractions with different denominators

Before adding or subtracting fractions with different denominators, rewrite them with a **common denominator**. A convenient choice is the least common denominator (LCD), the smallest positive number that both denominators divide into evenly.

### Worked example 5: Adding fractions with different denominators

Calculate:

\[
\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{2}
\]

Step 1: Find a common denominator. The denominators are 4 and 2; the least common denominator is 4.

Step 2: Rewrite \(\frac{1}{2}\) as an equivalent fraction with denominator 4.

\[
\frac{1}{2}=\frac{2}{4}
\]

Step 3: Add the numerators.

\[
\frac{3}{4}+\frac{2}{4}=\frac{5}{4}
\]

Final answer:

\[
\boxed{\frac{5}{4}}
\]

The result is an improper fraction because the numerator is larger than the denominator. That is acceptable. You can also write it as \(1\frac{1}{4}\), but the improper fraction is often more useful in algebra.

### Worked example 6: Subtracting fractions with different denominators

Calculate:

\[
\frac{5}{6}-\frac{1}{3}
\]

Step 1: Find a common denominator. The least common denominator of 6 and 3 is 6.

Step 2: Rewrite \(\frac{1}{3}\) with denominator 6.

\[
\frac{1}{3}=\frac{2}{6}
\]

Step 3: Subtract the numerators.

\[
\frac{5}{6}-\frac{2}{6}=\frac{3}{6}
\]

Step 4: Simplify by dividing the numerator and denominator by 3.

\[
\frac{3}{6}=\boxed{\frac{1}{2}}
\]

## 5. Multiplying and dividing fractions

Multiplication and division of fractions work differently from addition and subtraction. You do not need a common denominator for either operation.

### Multiplying fractions

Multiply the numerators together and multiply the denominators together. Then simplify.

\[
\frac{a}{b}\times\frac{c}{d}=\frac{ac}{bd}
\]

### Worked example 7: Multiplying fractions

Calculate:

\[
\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{3}{4}
\]

Step 1: Multiply the numerators: \(2\times3=6\).

Step 2: Multiply the denominators: \(3\times4=12\).

\[
\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{3}{4}=\frac{6}{12}
\]

Step 3: Simplify.

\[
\frac{6}{12}=\boxed{\frac{1}{2}}
\]

### Dividing fractions

To divide by a fraction, multiply by its **reciprocal**. The reciprocal is found by switching the numerator and denominator.

\[
\frac{a}{b}\div\frac{c}{d}=\frac{a}{b}\times\frac{d}{c}
\]

The divisor cannot be zero.

### Worked example 8: Dividing fractions

Calculate:

\[
\frac{2}{3}\div\frac{4}{5}
\]

Step 1: Keep the first fraction.

Step 2: Change division to multiplication.

Step 3: Flip the second fraction to get its reciprocal.

\[
\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{5}{4}
\]

Step 4: Multiply and simplify.

\[
\frac{2\times5}{3\times4}=\frac{10}{12}=\boxed{\frac{5}{6}}
\]

## 6. Bringing fractions and signed numbers into algebra

A **coefficient** is the number multiplying a variable. Coefficients can be positive, negative, whole numbers, or fractions.

For example, in \(-\frac{3}{4}x\), the coefficient is \(-\frac{3}{4}\).

The same like-term rule from Day 1 still applies: you can combine terms only when their variable parts match. When the coefficients are fractions, add or subtract those fractions and keep the variable part unchanged.

### Worked example 9: Combining fractional coefficients

Simplify:

\[
\frac{1}{2}x+\frac{1}{4}x
\]

Step 1: These are like terms because both contain \(x\).

Step 2: Add the coefficients. Use a common denominator of 4.

\[
\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}\right)x
=\left(\frac{2}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\right)x
\]

Step 3: Combine the fractions.

\[
\boxed{\frac{3}{4}x}
\]

### Worked example 10: Combining a negative and a positive coefficient

Simplify:

\[
-\frac{3}{4}y+\frac{1}{2}y
\]

Step 1: These are like terms, so combine the coefficients.

Step 2: Rewrite the fractions with a common denominator of 4.

\[
\left(-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{2}{4}\right)y
\]

Step 3: Add the signed fractions.

\[
-\frac{1}{4}y
\]

Final answer:

\[
\boxed{-\frac{1}{4}y}
\]

### Worked example 11: Evaluating an expression with a fraction

Evaluate the expression when \(x=8\):

\[
-\frac{1}{2}x+3
\]

Step 1: Substitute 8 for \(x\).

\[
-\frac{1}{2}(8)+3
\]

Step 2: Multiply.

\[
-4+3
\]

Step 3: Add the signed numbers.

\[
\boxed{-1}
\]

### Worked example 12: Distribution with a fractional coefficient

Expand and simplify:

\[
\frac{1}{2}(4x+6)
\]

Step 1: Distribute \(\frac{1}{2}\) to each term.

\[
\frac{1}{2}(4x)+\frac{1}{2}(6)
\]

Step 2: Multiply each term.

\[
2x+3
\]

Final answer:

\[
\boxed{2x+3}
\]

The distributive property works with fractions and negative numbers just as it does with whole numbers.

## 7. Common mistakes to avoid

| Common mistake | Correct approach |
|---|---|
| \(18\div(-3)=6\) | Different signs produce \(-6\). |
| \(\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{3}\) | Divide top and bottom by 3: \(\frac{3}{6}=\frac{1}{2}\). |
| \(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}=\frac{2}{5}\) | Find a common denominator: \(\frac{3}{6}+\frac{2}{6}=\frac{5}{6}\). |
| \(\frac{2}{3}\div\frac{4}{5}=\frac{8}{15}\) | Multiply by the reciprocal: \(\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{5}{4}=\frac{5}{6}\). |
| \(-\frac{3}{4}x+\frac{1}{2}x=-\frac{4}{6}x\) | Use a common denominator and combine carefully: \(-\frac{1}{4}x\). |
| \(\frac{1}{2}(4x+6)=2x+6\) | Distribute to both terms: \(2x+3\). |

Keep these reminders in your notes:

1. Different signs in multiplication or division produce a negative result; the same signs produce a positive result.
2. To subtract a signed number, add its opposite.
3. For fraction addition and subtraction, find a common denominator.
4. For fraction multiplication, multiply across; for division, multiply by the reciprocal.
5. Simplify fractions by dividing the numerator and denominator by the same common factor.

B. \(\frac{1}{4}x\)  
C. \(\frac{2}{4}x\)  
D. \(-\frac{4}{4}x\)

**7. What is \(\frac{1}{3}(6x+9)\)?**

A. \(2x+9\)  
B. \(6x+3\)  
C. \(2x+3\)  
D. \(6x+9\)

### Knowledge check answer key

1. **D — -5.** The signs differ, so subtract the absolute values: \(8-3=5\). Keep the sign of -8, which has the larger absolute value.
2. **B — -3.** Different signs in division produce a negative result, and \(12\div4=3\).
3. **C — \(\frac{1}{2}\).** Divide the numerator and denominator of \(\frac{3}{6}\) by 3.
4. **B — \(\frac{3}{4}\).** Rewrite \(\frac{1}{2}\) as \(\frac{2}{4}\), then add the numerators.
5. **C — \(\frac{2}{5}\).** Multiply across to get \(\frac{6}{15}\), then divide top and bottom by 3. Option D is equivalent but not simplified; the question asks for the simplified result.
6. **B — \(\frac{1}{4}x\).** Rewrite \(-\frac{1}{2}\) as \(-\frac{2}{4}\); then \(-\frac{2}{4}+\frac{3}{4}=\frac{1}{4}\).
7. **C — \(2x+3\).** Distribute \(\frac{1}{3}\) to both terms: \(\frac{1}{3}\cdot6x=2x\) and \(\frac{1}{3}\cdot9=3\).
