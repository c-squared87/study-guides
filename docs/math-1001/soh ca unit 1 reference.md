# Unit 1 Cheat Sheet — Exponents & Radicals

## 1. Properties of Exponents

| Property | Formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Product | $a^m \cdot a^n=a^{m+n}$ | $x^3\cdot x^4=x^7$ |
| Quotient | $\frac{a^m}{a^n}=a^{m-n}$ | $\frac{x^8}{x^3}=x^5$ |
| Power of a Power | $(a^m)^n=a^{mn}$ | $(x^3)^2=x^6$ |
| Power of a Product | $(ab)^n=a^nb^n$ | $(2x)^3=8x^3$ |
| Power of a Quotient | $\left(\frac ab\right)^n=\frac{a^n}{b^n}$ | $\left(\frac{x}{3}\right)^2=\frac{x^2}{9}$ |

**Rules to remember:**
- Multiply same bases → add exponents.
- Divide same bases → subtract exponents.
- Power raised to a power → multiply exponents.
- Apply powers to every factor inside parentheses.

---

## 2. Fractional Exponents

### Conversion Formulas

$$
a^{1/n}=\sqrt[n]{a}
$$

$$
\boxed{a^{m/n}=\sqrt[n]{a^m}}
$$

- Numerator = power
- Denominator = root index

| Fractional Exponent | Radical |
|---|---|
| $a^{1/2}$ | $\sqrt{a}$ |
| $a^{1/3}$ | $\sqrt[3]{a}$ |
| $a^{1/4}$ | $\sqrt[4]{a}$ |
| $a^{2/3}$ | $\sqrt[3]{a^2}$ |
| $a^{3/4}$ | $\sqrt[4]{a^3}$ |

### Examples

$$
27^{2/3}=(\sqrt[3]{27})^2=9
$$

$$
16^{3/4}=(\sqrt[4]{16})^3=8
$$

## 4. Simplifying Radicals

### Square Root Rule

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{a^2b}=a\sqrt{b}}
$$

Find the largest perfect-square factor, take its root outside, and leave the remaining factor inside.

### Steps

1. Find a perfect-square factor.
2. Rewrite the radicand as a product.
3. Separate the radicals.
4. Simplify the perfect square.

### Examples

$$
\sqrt{48}=\sqrt{16\cdot3}=4\sqrt3
$$

$$
\sqrt{180}=\sqrt{36\cdot5}=6\sqrt5
$$

$$
\sqrt{495}=\sqrt{9\cdot55}=3\sqrt{55}
$$

---

## 5. Product Property of Radicals

$$
\boxed{\sqrt[n]{a}\cdot\sqrt[n]{b}=\sqrt[n]{ab}}
$$

Radicals with the same index can be combined by multiplying inside.

**Example:**

$$
\sqrt5\cdot\sqrt{20}=\sqrt{100}=10
$$

---

## 6. Quotient Property of Radicals

$$
\boxed{\frac{\sqrt[n]{a}}{\sqrt[n]{b}}=\sqrt[n]{\frac ab}}
$$

Radicals with the same index can be combined by dividing inside.

**Example:**

$$
\frac{\sqrt{18}}{\sqrt2}=\sqrt9=3
$$

### Quotient with a Fraction

$$
\boxed{\sqrt{\frac ab}=\frac{\sqrt a}{\sqrt b}}
$$

**Example:**

$$
\sqrt{\frac{36}{49}}=\frac67
$$

---

## 7. Variables Inside Radicals

$$
\sqrt{x^2}=|x|
$$

$$
\sqrt{x^4}=x^2
$$

$$
\sqrt{x^6}=x^3
$$

$$
\sqrt{x^5}=x^2\sqrt{x}
$$

**Rule:** For square roots, pairs of variable factors can come outside the radical.

## 8. Quick Decision Guide

| Problem | Rule |
|---|---|
| Same base multiplied | Add exponents |
| Same base divided | Subtract exponents |
| Power raised to a power | Multiply exponents |
| Product raised to a power | Apply to every factor |
| Fraction raised to a power | Apply to numerator & denominator |
| Fractional exponent | Convert to radical |
| Radical with perfect-square factor | Extract square root |
| Radicals multiplied | Product Property |
| Radicals divided | Quotient Property |

---

## Common Mistakes

- Do not add exponents when bases are different.
- Do not forget to apply powers to coefficients and every variable.
- The denominator of a fractional exponent is the root index.
- Check that factors multiply back to the original radicand.
- Do not pull a variable out of a radical unless its exponent allows it.
- Always simplify radicals completely.
