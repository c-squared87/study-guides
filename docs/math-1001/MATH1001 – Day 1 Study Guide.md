## 2. Understanding algebraic expressions

### What is an algebraic expression?

An algebraic expression is a mathematical phrase containing numbers, variables, and mathematical operations.

For example:

\[
3x+5
\]

This expression contains:

| Component | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Variable | A letter representing a number | \(x\) |
| Coefficient | A number multiplied by a variable | \(3\) |
| Constant | A number without a variable | \(5\) |
| Term | A component separated by addition or subtraction | \(3x\), \(5\) |

Let's look at another example:

\[
7x-4+2y
\]

This expression has three terms:

- \(7x\)
- \(-4\)
- \(2y\)

Notice that the negative sign belongs to the term. The second term is negative four, not positive four.

### Important distinction: Expressions vs. equations

These two concepts are easy to confuse.

An **expression** represents a mathematical quantity:

\[
3x+5
\]

An **equation** states that two quantities are equal:

\[
3x+5=20
\]

The equals sign is what distinguishes an equation from an expression.

You can simplify an expression, evaluate an expression, or solve an equation.

### Worked example 1: Identify the components

Consider:

\[
5x+8
\]

Identify the following:

- **Variable:** \(x\)
- **Coefficient:** \(5\)
- **Constant:** \(8\)
- **Number of terms:** 2

Now consider:

\[
-3x+4y-9
\]

- **Variables:** \(x\) and \(y\)
- **Coefficients:** \(-3\) and \(4\)
- **Constant:** \(-9\)
- **Number of terms:** 3

**A small but important detail:** When a variable appears by itself, its coefficient is 1.

For example:

\[
x=1x
\]

And:

\[
-x=-1x
\]

This becomes important when combining like terms.

## 3. Combining like terms

This is the concept you encountered in Question 13 of your diagnostic.

### What are like terms?

Like terms are terms that have exactly the same variable component, including the same exponents.

For example:

\[
3x+5x
\]

These are like terms because both contain \(x\) raised to the first power.

You can combine them:

\[
3x+5x=8x
\]

Think of it as adding quantities of the same type.

If you have three apples and five apples, you have eight apples.

You wouldn't combine three apples and five oranges into eight apples. They're different things.

The same principle applies to algebra.

### Examples of like and unlike terms

| Expression | Like terms? | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| \(3x\) and \(5x\) | Yes | Same variable |
| \(4y\) and \(2y\) | Yes | Same variable |
| \(7\) and \(3\) | Yes | Both constants |
| \(3x\) and \(3y\) | No | Different variables |
| \(2x\) and \(2x^2\) | No | Different exponents |
| \(5xy\) and \(3xy\) | Yes | Same variables and exponents |

### The rule for combining like terms

Add or subtract the coefficients. Keep the variable portion unchanged.

\[
3x+5x=(3+5)x=8x
\]

The variable doesn't change. Only the coefficients change.

### Worked example 2: Combining like terms

Simplify:

\[
4x+7x-3x
\]

**Step 1: Identify the like terms.**

All three terms contain \(x\).

**Step 2: Combine their coefficients.**

\[
(4+7-3)x
\]

**Step 3: Simplify.**

\[
\boxed{8x}
\]

### Worked example 3: Variables and constants

Simplify:

\[
5x+3+2x+7
\]

**Step 1: Group the like terms.**

\[
(5x+2x)+(3+7)
\]

**Step 2: Combine the variable terms.**

\[
7x
\]

**Step 3: Combine the constants.**

\[
10
\]

Final answer:

\[
\boxed{7x+10}
\]

Notice that we did not combine \(7x\) and \(10\). They are unlike terms.

### Worked example 4: Negative coefficients

Simplify:

\[
8x-3x+2-9
\]

Separate the variable terms and constants:

\[
(8x-3x)+(2-9)
\]

Combine:

\[
5x-7
\]

Final answer:

\[
\boxed{5x-7}
\]

Remember that subtraction is the same as adding a negative number:

\[
2-9=2+(-9)=-7
\]

This is a common source of errors, so pay attention to negative signs.

## 4. The distributive property

This was the concept you marked as unfamiliar in Question 14.

It is one of the most important algebraic rules you'll learn.

### What is the distributive property?

The distributive property allows us to multiply a number or variable by every term inside a set of parentheses.

The general formula is:

\[
a(b+c)=ab+ac
\]

The number outside the parentheses is multiplied by every term inside.

Think of the outside number as distributing itself across the entire expression.

### Worked example 5: Basic distribution

Simplify:

\[
2(3x+4)
\]

**Step 1: Multiply the outside number by the first term.**

\[
2\times3x=6x
\]

**Step 2: Multiply the outside number by the second term.**

\[
2\times4=8
\]

**Step 3: Combine the results.**

\[
\boxed{6x+8}
\]

That's it!

### Worked example 6: Distribution with subtraction

Simplify:

\[
3(4x-5)
\]

**Step 1: Distribute the 3.**

\[
(3\times4x)-(3\times5)
\]

**Step 2: Multiply.**

\[
12x-15
\]

Final answer:

\[
\boxed{12x-15}
\]

### Worked example 7: Distribution with negative numbers

Simplify:

\[
-2(3x-4)
\]

**Step 1: Multiply the negative 2 by the first term.**

\[
-2\times3x=-6x
\]

**Step 2: Multiply the negative 2 by the second term.**

\[
-2\times(-4)=+8
\]

**Step 3: Combine.**

\[
\boxed{-6x+8}
\]

**Watch your signs!** A negative multiplied by a negative produces a positive.

### Worked example 8: Distribution followed by combining like terms

Now let's combine both concepts.

Simplify:

\[
2(3x+4)+5x
\]

**Step 1: Apply the distributive property.**

\[
6x+8+5x
\]

**Step 2: Identify like terms.**

\[
6x+5x+8
\]

**Step 3: Combine.**

\[
\boxed{11x+8}
\]

This is particularly important because algebra problems frequently require multiple operations to simplify an expression.

## 5. Common mistakes to avoid

These are worth keeping in your notes.

| Incorrect approach | Correct approach |
|---|---|
| \(3x+5=8x\) | Unlike terms cannot be combined |
| \(4x+2x=6x^2\) | \(4x+2x=6x\) |
| \(2(3x+4)=6x+4\) | \(2(3x+4)=6x+8\) |
| \(-3(x-2)=-3x-6\) | \(-3(x-2)=-3x+6\) |
| \(5x+3x+2=10x\) | \(5x+3x+2=8x+2\) |

The two most important things to remember:

1. Only combine like terms.
2. When distributing, multiply every term inside the parentheses.

## 9. Knowledge check – Answer key and explanations

**1. Correct answer: A — \(3x+5\)**  
An expression does not contain an equals sign. The other choices are equations or equality statements.

**2. Correct answer: C — -7**  
The coefficient is the signed number multiplying the variable. Here it is -7.

**3. Correct answer: C — \(5xy\) and \(2xy\)**  
Both terms contain exactly the same variable component, \(xy\).

**4. Correct answer: A — \(5x\)**  
Combine the coefficients: \(4+3-2=5\).

**5. Correct answer: B — \(6x+15\)**  
Distribute the 3 to both terms: \(3\times2x=6x\) and \(3\times5=15\).

**6. Correct answer: C — \(-2x+8\)**  
Multiply both terms by -2. The product of two negative numbers is positive.

**7. Correct answer: C — No, they are unlike terms.**  
A variable term and a constant are unlike terms, so they cannot be combined.
